New Game
Chrono Cross uses a unique stat-growth system instead of traditional experience points. Regular battles grant small stat increases, but only up to a cap; once battles stop giving gains, you've hit it. Larger, permanent stat boosts come only from defeating specific "boss" fights, each of which awards a numbered level-up star (tracked throughout this guide). All active party members must survive a boss fight to get its full stat bonus. Characters sitting outside the active party miss out on stat gains entirely until the Tele-Porter key item allows swapping them in.

13) Fort Dragonia [Another World]
Stock up on Iceberg, HolyLight, Inferno, and Volcano traps before entering; none of these will be purchasable again for quite a while after this dungeon, and they're all useful against upcoming bosses.
A Dragoon guards the entrance and can be fought if the Water Dragon Isle detour (and its FrogPrince summon) was skipped; defeating it drops the FrogPrince and awards the 15th level-up star. It doesn't appear on New Game+.
The main objective across this floor is deactivating a series of colored obelisks (Red, Yellow, Green, Blue, and White), each behind its own puzzle room; a final Black destination is unlocked by defeating its guardian boss directly rather than a separate deactivation step.
Fort Dragonia: Red Room
Whatever direction the statue faces represents north; from there, pick the direction that would extend over the correct hole (per the room's own diagram: if the statue faces north, choose east; if south, choose west, and so on). Only the northern hole leads to enemy encounters (Cybots); there are infinite retries if the wrong hole is chosen.
The four holes also contain a Volcano Trap, Flame Charm, MagmaBurst, and Fire Pillar.
Once the pillar is found, the pattern in the branch room lights up to confirm it. Head to the Yellow Room next.
Fort Dragonia: Yellow Room
How the maze itself moves depends on current party formation. The exact starting order (and so the exact fix needed) also depends on formation; use Select outside to swap leaders, and the glowing blue pad there to shift the order further, until the Head-Body-Tail arrangement is reached. Inspect the pedestal again afterward to reconfigure the maze.
Earth Charm: from a chest reachable in the Body-Tail-Head order.
ElectroBolt: from a chest reachable via stairs, lowered by a blue switch inside the maze, in the Tail-Head-Body order.

Trigger the blue switch along the way to lower a stairway shortcut back. Save with any loot collected before continuing on.
BOSS: Taurusoid
Innate: Green   HP: 1600   Spoils: @Carapace, Iron Vest
It heals itself with HealAll if hit by yellow elements, so stick to normal attacks and techs; a Weaken helps since it otherwise deals around 70 damage per hit. After its first three turns, it randomly settles into one of two attack routines, one manageable and one considerably more hectic with frequent double-tech chains; try to avoid letting the field effect go fully green, which seems to encourage the harder routine. The "angry" mode unleashes three attacks in a row, so heal up if it appears; Imbecile is worth applying here too, since its damage output rises as the field effect turns white. @Screw is stealable. Innate: Red   HP: 400   Spoils: n/a

This two-phase fight is a great source of Inferno and Volcano elements, but only during the red-innate first phase; trap them before it transitions, since they can't be recovered afterward, and the fight can be fled and retried to farm extra copies before finishing it off. The second (black) phase uses a trappable FreeFall; BlackHole Trap is also stealable. MeteorShower, HolyLight, and Luminaire cut through its HP quickly, with periodic HealAlls to offset DevilThunda. Innate: Yellow   HP: 820   Spoils: Dragoon Gauntlet, Mythril Mail

BOSS: Lynx
Innate: Black   HP: 1000   Steal: Pendragon Sigil B, Sky Djinn Ring

His only black attack, GlideHook, can deal 100+ damage and occasionally strikes three or four times in one turn, hitting Serge especially hard. Keep Serge above 150 HP and everyone else above 100 at all times; bombard him with trapped elements while using HiRes/BatEye for support. 666G awarded, plus a 21st level-up star.

BOSS: TimeDevourer
Innate: variable   HP: 15000   Spoils: Earring of Hope
Traps set during one form carry over to the next. TimeDevourer absorbs whichever innate color it currently is, so summoners should be careful. All allies take damage whenever it changes form and innate, usually 50 to 70, though sometimes none at all.
This is a lengthy fight with a huge spread of trappable elements across every color. Its element lineup changes with each form shift, which makes preparation somewhat easier, but Diminish remains the single best approach throughout, even if repetitive, given the 15,000 HP total. Physical attacks are reliable in every phase.
White (first form): This opening form only uses physical attacks, hitting the whole party the way a Rod or Boomerang user would; individually weak, but it adds up. Conserve elements with an attack-attack-defend pattern, though Diminish is still fine to use. The field starts mostly black, limiting incoming damage. A HolyHealing element can be stolen during this form.
Yellow: The field effect is kept deliberately randomized here through three weak, status-free elements. It eventually settles into ThundaStorm once, then OmegaYellow from then on, so try to get through this phase before then (GreenField plus Sonja can help). Serge deals extra damage since this form isn't yellow-innate; if a Pendragon Sigil is equipped, its Bushwhacker can trigger a "TurnGreen" effect on Serge, roughly doubling his physical damage.
Red: If Serge has a Pendragon Sigil A equipped, this form's three-element combo can trigger a "TurnBlue" effect on him instead, similarly doubling physical damage. It uses Volcano and OmegaRed, neither worth trapping; instead, save up for the coming HolyHealings by Diminishing the field and healing with HealAll, or CureAll if a blue-innate character is available.
Green: This form hits harder and drops yellow elements entirely, taking the Pendragon Sigil trick off the table. Aim for a matching Field element and pop a summon while Serge continues attacking. Not too difficult, though the party will take more consistent damage than in the calmer forms.
Blue: Keep Revives ready, since this form mixes in the ever-annoying HellBound alongside blue and white elements, and its physical attacks hit the whole party. This form is annoying, but the next one is even more so; consider setting a BlackHole trap here in preparation for the next form.
Black: This pattern is only five turns long, making a BlackHole use likely and dangerous, since it can inflict an instant KO. Set a trap for it and be ready to replace the trap if it's used, while Serge keeps up physical pressure. As long as BlackHole doesn't land, this form is a bit easier to manage. A Black Plate on Serge helps considerably if he can draw its attacks; this phase shouldn't last long if hits are connecting. An EagleEye element can help speed the form along.
White (final form): An UltraNova trap works well here, since HolyLight becomes its main attack; expect some Fatigued status effects regardless. This form leans more physical than the middle stretch of the fight, so Weaken and BatEye help while Serge continues attacking with his blade. Keep HealAll ready whenever UltraNova is close, to keep the damage in a manageable range.
